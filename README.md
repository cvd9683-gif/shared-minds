# Shared Minds

Weekly assignments for ITP's Shared Minds class. Cathy Doss.

The index page is [`index.html`](index.html) — open it in a browser, or serve the folder
with `python3 -m http.server`.

---

## Week 1 — Thought Forest

Type a thought and it takes root. Each one becomes a small procedural tree that grows into
a swaying forest, so a stream of consciousness ends up as something you can stand in and
look at. An **Overwhelm** slider drives how hard the whole forest moves.

**[Open the forest](https://cvd9683-gif.github.io/shared-minds-thought-forest/)**
· [Source](https://github.com/cvd9683-gif/shared-minds-thought-forest)

## Week 2 — Learning the Steps, Feeling the Dance

Take turns with a dancer. You teach it a phrase of four to eight moves using the arrow keys
and the space bar, in whatever rhythm you like; it answers with a phrase of its own. A
language model makes that answer from nothing but your move names and the millisecond gaps
between them — it never sees the figure, or you — and both phrases are drawn at the same
scale so one can be read against the other.

**[Teach the dancer](https://shared-minds-learning-the-steps.vercel.app/)**
· [Source](https://github.com/cvd9683-gif/shared-minds-learning-the-steps)

The dancer really answers — a language model reads your rhythm and dances back. A few turns
each, since it runs on a class budget.

## Week 3 — Do You See What I Mean?

Pictionary, except you paint with your face. You get a simple prompt and 90 seconds; your
webcam watches your head and face, and those movements steer the brush. Afterwards your face
and your painting replay side by side, in sync — the machine measured every expression, but
did it ever understand what you were trying to draw?

**[Paint with your face](https://cvd9683-gif.github.io/do-you-see-what-i-mean/)**
· [Source](https://github.com/cvd9683-gif/do-you-see-what-i-mean)

## Week 4 — Music Map

A song has a history before it becomes part of yours. Move through tracks by the date they
were saved, then open one into a map of the recordings, samples, interpolations and people
connected to it. Each route is recorded as a journey — JSON saved under your name to
localStorage, or Firebase once configured — so you can return to the original track, listen
again, and note what you hear now. Connects to Spotify for your saved tracks and search.

**[Open the map](week-4-music-map/site/)**
